## Register

German `du`, French `vous`, Spanish `tú`, Brazilian Portuguese `você`, Italian `tu`.

Japanese uses the polite です/ます form in sentences and plain noun phrases on buttons and titles. Katakana loanwords follow Japanese TV apps (チャンネル, ガイド only in 番組ガイド-style compounds, プロフィール); 番組 is a programme.

Chinese uses a neutral, polite register with no 您/你 mixing: Simplified uses 你 as mainland apps do; Taiwan and Hong Kong use 你. Mainland vocabulary for Simplified (频道, 节目, 视频, 设置, 屏幕); Taiwan vocabulary for Traditional (頻道, 節目, 影片, 設定, 螢幕, 軟體, 網路, 登入); Hong Kong vocabulary for Hong Kong (屏幕, 軟件, 網絡, 個人檔案).

## Rules

- Times are 24-hour outside English; dates put the day first in the European languages and the month first, with 月 and 日, in Japanese and Chinese.
- Counts use the language's plural forms, or a label form ("Kanäle: 3") that reads correctly for any number. Japanese and Chinese have one form and use counters (3 チャンネル, 3 个频道, 3 個頻道).
- Keep placeholders such as `{name}` exactly as they are.
- Capital `ẞ` isn't in the bundled fonts: write `SS` in upper-case text.
- Keep key hints (`OK`, `ESC`, `▲▼`) as they are; only translate the words.
- Japanese and Chinese use full-width punctuation in sentences (。、！？「」（）：) and 、 between list items. A space separates CJK text from a Latin word or number only where the source has one (`CM 枠` is written that way on purpose; `3 チャンネル` keeps its space).
- After a Japanese or Chinese change, run `python3 tool/subset_cjk_font.py` so the bundled font has every character.
